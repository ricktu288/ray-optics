WebGPU cooperative-ray and CPU benchmark

This runs a small multi-bounce 2D ray simulation. It compares one invocation per ray with multiple workgroup lanes or one hardware subgroup per ray. The cooperative paths include closest-hit reduction, synchronization, hit broadcast, reflection, and the next traversal.

Direct variants scan every curve without a BVH. BVH4 cooperative variants divide disjoint BVH subtrees between lanes. All variants execute the same exact ray/segment intersection and reflection code. Every measured bounce also includes production-shaped active-queue weight, prefix, and fill collector passes. Scene and BVH building are excluded from timings for both CPU and GPU.

Coherent rays start close together with similar directions; incoherent rays spread their origins and directions across the scene. Both are measured because scalar BVH traversal benefits from neighboring rays taking similar paths, while cooperative BVH lanes deliberately traverse different partitions of the same ray.

Scalar and workgroup methods match the cooperation mechanisms available in production. Hardware-subgroup methods are experimental and are reported when the adapter and browser expose the required features. A JavaScript CPU BVH4 implementation is included as a wall-clock baseline. This page only measures and exports results; it does not modify engine tuning.

The default experiment is one matrix containing every power-of-four combination from 1 through 4,096 curves and from 1 through 16,384 rays. Direct and BVH4 strategies are compared at scalar and 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 workgroup lanes per ray. Experimental subgroup strategies are added when supported.
